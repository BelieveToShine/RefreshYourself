RBAC — permissions flow down, they never flow up

"What is RBAC on Azure — roles, scope, and the least-privilege principle behind assigning them?"

Why it matters ▾
  • The natural next question once an interview moves from "who is this" (Microsoft Entra ID, managed identities) to "what can they actually do."
  • Explains a real support ticket: someone removed from a team's group can still see or edit a resource, because a separate assignment sitting at a higher scope is still granting access.
  • A near-universal opener the moment security or governance comes up on any Azure team.
🔥 Easy interview recall

Role → what you can do

Scope → where it applies

Assignment → role + principal, glued to that scope

Think: effective access = the union of every assignment at or above your scope — and it only flows down.

🧠 Visual Mental Model — understand it in 5 seconds
⚠️ Common Trap

A narrower assignment at a lower scope can "block" a broader one from above. It can't — a subscription-level Owner assignment flows down into every resource group and resource inside it, and nothing you do at the resource level can revoke that inherited access.

🔄 Likely Follow-up

"What's the least-privilege practice here?" Assign the narrowest built-in role at the narrowest scope that lets someone actually do their job, rather than defaulting to Owner or Contributor at the subscription level.

🎯 Say this in the interview — the exact lines to land

"Azure RBAC access is the union of every role assigned at or above your scope, and it only flows downward — never up."

  • Role + principal + scope → the three pieces every assignment is built from.
  • Union, not override → a narrower assignment down at a resource can never block or revoke a broader one from above.
  • Least privilege → pick the narrowest built-in role at the narrowest scope that still gets the job done.
📖 Explanation ▾

Azure RBAC assigns a role — a bundle of permissions such as Contributor, Reader, or Storage Blob Data Reader — to a security principal (a user, a group, a service principal, or a managed identity) at a scope. Scope is one of four levels, from broadest to narrowest: management group, subscription, resource group, or a single resource.

Effective access for any principal at any resource is the union of every role assignment that applies at or above that resource in the scope chain — every assignment made higher up flows straight down into everything nested inside it. There's no mechanism to unassign or override an inherited permission from a lower scope; access only ever adds up as you move down, it never subtracts.

💻 See the code ▾
Azure CLI 
az role assignment create \
    --assignee <principal-id> \
    --role "Storage Blob Data Reader" \
    --scope /subscriptions/<sub-id>/resourceGroups/rg-demo   -- scoped to one RG, not the subscription

This grants <principal-id> read access to blob data in rg-demo only — nothing else in the subscription is touched.

🌍 Real-world example ▾

Scenario: a CI/CD pipeline needs to deploy resources automatically. Its service principal gets Contributor scoped only to one resource group — not the whole subscription — so if the pipeline's credentials are ever compromised, the damage is contained to that one resource group and can't touch anything else in the subscription.

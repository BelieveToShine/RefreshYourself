Handing over the real variable — three sets of rules

Why it matters ▾
  • Explains how a method can hand back more than one result without a wrapper object.
  • Tells you exactly what the compiler will and won't let you get away with for each keyword.
  • A sharp way to show you understand C# beyond "everything is pass by value."
📖 Explanation ▾

Normally, C# passes a copy — the method gets its own version, and changes inside it never reach the caller. ref, out, and in all skip that copy and hand over the real variable instead — they just disagree on what the method may do with it.

ref needs a value going in and allows changing it. out doesn't care what's there going in, but the compiler forces the method to assign it before it returns — perfect for "give me a second result" (like int.TryParse). in only reads — the compiler blocks any write, which makes it useful purely for performance: skip copying a big struct without risking it gets changed underneath you.

🌍 Real-world example ▾

Handing someone your actual notebook. ref hands over your notebook already written in — they can read it and add to it. out hands over a blank notebook — they're required to write something in it before giving it back. in hands over your notebook with a "look, don't write" rule.

🎯 Say this in the interview — the exact lines to land

  • All three pass the real variable, not a copy.
  • ref = must be initialized, may read + write.
  • out = may start uninitialized, method must write it.
  • in = read-only, purely to skip copying a large struct.
  • out is how methods return more than one value without a wrapper type.

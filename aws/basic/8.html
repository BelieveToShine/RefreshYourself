RDS — a read replica is not high availability

"Multi-AZ vs. Read Replica — what's the actual difference, and can a read replica provide high availability on its own?"

Why it matters ▾
🔁 Multi-AZ
❗ ProblemWhen the AZ hosting the primary goes down, there's nothing already in sync and ready to take over — the database stays down until someone manually rebuilds it.
✅ SolutionMulti-AZ keeps a synchronously replicated standby in another AZ that RDS automatically fails over to, typically within one to two minutes, with no action needed from anyone.
RecallMulti-AZ → automatic failover standby, not extra read capacity.
📖 Read Replica
❗ ProblemHeavy reporting or analytics queries competing with regular checkout/app traffic on the primary can slow the whole app down for everyone.
✅ SolutionA read replica is an asynchronous copy that takes that read traffic off the primary — it can be promoted to standalone during a disaster, but that's a manual step with replication-lag risk, not automatic protection.
RecallRead Replica → scales reads; promotion during a disaster is manual, not HA.
🔥 Easy interview recall

Multi-AZ → automatic failover standby

Read Replica → scales reads, not HA

Think: Multi-AZ is a spare tire that swaps itself on. Read Replica is a second cashier for a busy checkout line.

🧠 Visual Mental Model — understand it in 5 seconds
⚠️ Common Trap

"We have a read replica, so we have high availability." A read replica alone gives you no automatic failover — it can lag behind the primary, and turning it into a usable primary during an outage is a manual step you trigger yourself, not something RDS does for you the way Multi-AZ does.

🔄 Likely Follow-up

"When would you choose Aurora over standard RDS?" When you need higher read-replica scaling (up to 15 low-lag replicas vs. RDS's fewer, higher-lag ones), faster failover, or Aurora's storage layer that decouples compute from a shared, self-healing storage layer.

🎯 Say this in the interview — the exact lines to land

"Multi-AZ is for automatic failover — a synchronously replicated standby in another AZ that RDS switches to on its own if the primary fails. A read replica is for scaling reads — an asynchronous copy that can lag, so a read replica alone is not high availability."

  • Multi-AZ → sync standby, automatic failover, not used for day-to-day reads/writes.
  • Read replica → async copy, offloads read traffic, exists to scale reads, not primarily for HA.
  • Manual promotion → a replica can be promoted to standalone during a disaster, but that's a manual step with replication-lag risk, not automatic protection.
  • Use both together → Multi-AZ for failover protection, read replicas for read scaling — two different problems, two different features.
📖 Explanation ▾

Multi-AZ creates a standby copy of the primary database in a different Availability Zone, kept in sync synchronously — every write to the primary is confirmed on the standby before it's considered done. The standby isn't used for reads or writes day to day; its only job is to be ready. If RDS detects the primary has failed, it automatically redirects the database's endpoint to the standby, typically within one to two minutes, with no action needed from anyone.

A read replica is a separate copy built to take read traffic off the primary — reporting queries, analytics, a read-heavy service — and it's kept in sync asynchronously, so it can genuinely fall behind under load. A read replica can live in the same Region or a different one, and RDS lets you manually promote one to a standalone, writable instance. That promotion can work in a disaster, but it's a decision and an action someone has to take, and whatever lag existed at that moment becomes lost or delayed data — nothing like the automatic, zero-lag guarantee Multi-AZ gives.

🧭 Use Cases — recognize these when they happen to you ▾
1 The AZ hosting your primary database goes down

RDS detects the failure and redirects the database's endpoint to the already-in-sync standby on its own — no one is paged for it, and no manual step is needed. This is the #1 reason Multi-AZ exists.

RememberAZ outage → Multi-AZ fails over automatically, no manual step.
2 A reporting dashboard's heavy queries are competing with checkout traffic on the primary

A read replica takes the reporting/analytics queries off the primary entirely, so checkout traffic stops competing with them — the trade-off is that the replica's data can genuinely lag behind under load, since it's kept in sync asynchronously.

RememberRead-heavy load → add a replica, not more failover.
3 You only have a read replica and assume that alone covers high availability

A read replica alone gives no automatic failover — it can lag behind the primary, and turning it into a usable primary during an outage is a manual step someone has to trigger, not something RDS does for you the way Multi-AZ does. A great interview trap — candidates often say "we use read replicas" when asked about availability.

RememberReplica ≠ HA — promotion is manual, with lag risk.
🌍 Real-world example ▾

A team enables Multi-AZ on their order database purely so an AZ outage doesn't take the app down — RDS fails over automatically, no one is paged for it. Separately, they add two read replicas so the reporting dashboard's heavy queries stop competing with checkout traffic on the primary. Same database, two unrelated features solving two unrelated problems.

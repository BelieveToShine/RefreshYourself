A longer context window doesn't mean equal recall — the "lost in the middle" effect

"If a model supports a huge context window, does it actually use everything in that window equally well?"

Why it matters ▾
  • Comes up whenever long-context models or RAG design get discussed — it separates candidates who treat context length as just a token limit from those who know it's also a placement problem.
  • Explains a real production symptom: an answer that misses a fact that's clearly somewhere in the prompt, even though the context window was large enough to hold it.
  • Sets up the follow-up almost every interviewer asks next: why retrieval and reranking still matter even with a huge context window.
🔥 Easy interview recall

Bigger window → fits more text, not equal recall

"Lost in the middle" → recall dips for buried content

Fix → put the important text near the start or end

Think: recall across a long prompt is shaped like a U — high at both edges, low in the middle.

🧠 Visual Mental Model — understand it in 5 seconds
⚠️ Common Trap

"A huge context window means I can dump everything in and stop worrying about order." Recall reliably drops for whatever sits in the middle of a long prompt, even though every token is technically "in context" — position still decides what the model actually uses well.

🔄 Likely Follow-up

"If position matters this much, why not just always max out the context window with everything retrieved?" Volume alone doesn't fix placement — a few well-ranked passages placed at the start or end are more likely to actually be used than a huge dump where the best one lands in the middle; that's exactly why retrieval and reranking still matter even with a huge context window.

🎯 Say this in the interview — the exact lines to land

"A bigger context window lets you fit far more text, but it doesn't fix recall — models reliably recall content near the start or end of a long prompt better than content buried in the middle, the well-documented 'lost in the middle' effect, so where you place the important text still matters."

  • Long context window → fits far more text, doesn't guarantee even recall across it.
  • "Lost in the middle" → recall for buried content is noticeably worse than at the edges.
  • Placement fix → put the question and the best passages near the start or end, not buried in the middle.
  • Why retrieval/reranking still matter → a few well-placed passages beat dumping everything into a giant context.
📖 Explanation ▾

Long-context models extend the usable context window substantially, so a single prompt can now hold whole documents, long conversation histories, or many retrieved passages that older, smaller-context models simply couldn't fit at all. But being able to fit something in the window isn't the same as the model using every part of it equally well.

This is the "lost in the middle" effect — a well-documented finding that models recall and use information placed near the very start or the very end of a long context noticeably better than information buried in the middle, even though technically everything in the window is "in context." Skim a long email and it's the same pattern: the opening and the sign-off stick, the request in paragraph seven is the one that gets missed.

The practical implication: don't assume position inside a long prompt is free. Put the actual question and the most relevant retrieved passages near the edges — start or end — rather than burying them in the middle, especially as the prompt gets longer. It's also why retrieval and reranking (picking the best few passages, not dumping in everything retrieved) still earn their place even with a huge context window — a giant context full of mediocre placement isn't automatically as effective as a short one with the best passages sitting where the model actually attends well.

💻 See the code ▾
Pseudocode 
# ❌ best passage buried in the middle
context = "\n\n".join([passage_2, passage_4, best_passage, passage_1, passage_3])
prompt = f"{context}\n\n{question}"

The single most relevant passage sits third out of five — right where "lost in the middle" hits hardest.

Pseudocode 
# ✅ best passage moved to an edge, question stays at the very end
context = "\n\n".join([best_passage, passage_1, passage_2, passage_3, passage_4])
prompt = f"{context}\n\n{question}"

Same five passages, same question — only the order changed, moving the one that matters to an edge the model reliably attends to well.

🌍 Real-world example ▾

Skimming a long email is the same pattern: you remember the greeting and the sign-off clearly, but a request buried in paragraph seven is the one that gets missed and has to be repeated.

Scenario: a RAG app stuffs fifteen retrieved passages into a very long prompt, and the model still misses a fact that's clearly in there somewhere. The likely cause: that fact landed in the middle of the stuffed context — moving the top two or three passages (by relevance) to the very start or end of the prompt, instead of relying on raw volume, is the fix worth trying first.

Who's allowed to see in? Four rings of visibility

Why it matters ▾
  • Explains the compile error "is inaccessible due to its protection level" before it confuses you.
  • Tells you how to design a class that hides its internals properly.
  • A five-second question interviewers use to check real understanding, not memorized syntax.
📖 Explanation ▾

Think of it as nested rooms, smallest to biggest. private is a locked room only the class itself can enter. protected opens that door to subclasses too. internal opens it to the whole assembly (project), regardless of class hierarchy. public has no door at all.

protected internal and private protected combine two rings — "subclasses OR same assembly" vs. "subclasses that are ALSO in the same assembly." They're rare, but knowing they exist signals you've gone past the basics.

Default access (no modifier written) depends on context — private for class members, internal for a top-level class — so leaving it blank isn't "no restriction," it's a specific, easy-to-forget default.

🌍 Real-world example ▾

A house. private is your bedroom — just you. protected is shared with family (subclasses). internal is the whole street having a key (same assembly), regardless of who's related to whom. public is a shop with its front door wide open.

🎯 Say this in the interview — the exact lines to land

  • private → this class only. public → everyone.
  • protected → this class + subclasses, any assembly.
  • internal → anywhere in this assembly, any class.
  • protected internal = subclasses OR same assembly — broader than either alone.
  • Default access differs by context — never assume "blank" means "wide open."
